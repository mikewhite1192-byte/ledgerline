# Harrow Flats Trucking Driver Pay Policy

SAMPLE. Harrow Flats Trucking is a fictitious carrier based in Pueblo, Colorado, created for a
software demonstration. Every name, amount and rule in this document is invented.

# 1. Purpose and scope

1.1 This policy explains how Harrow Flats Trucking pays its company drivers and its leased
owner-operators, what it deducts from a settlement, and how a driver can question a settlement. It
takes effect on November 2, 2026 and replaces every earlier pay letter.

1.2 Where a driver's signed lease or offer letter sets a different rate for that driver, the signed
document wins for that rate only. Everything else in this policy still applies.

# 2. Pay week and pay day

2.1 The pay week runs from 12:00 a.m. Monday through 11:59 p.m. Sunday, Mountain time. All dates and
times in this policy and on settlement statements are Mountain time.

2.2 Settlements are paid by direct deposit on the Friday after the pay week closes. A load counts in
the pay week in which it is delivered with a signed bill of lading.

# 3. How drivers are paid

3.1 Company drivers on long-haul lanes are paid $0.62 per loaded mile. Company drivers on local
routes are paid a flat $200 per load.

3.2 Leased owner-operators are paid 74 percent of the linehaul and fuel surcharge on each load they
haul. Accessorial charges are not part of that base; they are paid separately under section 4.

3.3 Empty miles driven under dispatch are paid to per-mile drivers at half the loaded rate. Miles
are measured from the practical route, not the odometer.

# 4. Accessorial pay and reimbursements

4.1 When a customer pays a detention, layover or extra-stop charge on a load, the driver who hauled
the load receives 75 percent of that charge on the settlement for the week the customer is billed.

4.2 Lumper fees and tolls paid by the driver are reimbursed at cost when the receipt is turned in
with the trip paperwork. Reimbursements are not shared and are not counted as pay.

# 5. Occupational accident insurance

5.1 Leased owner-operators are covered under the carrier's occupational accident policy. The carrier
charges each owner-operator $10.50 for each calendar day on which the owner-operator is under a
dispatched load, including days spent waiting at a shipper or receiver under that load.

5.2 Company drivers are covered under workers' compensation at no charge to the driver.

# 6. Escrow

6.1 Each leased owner-operator keeps an escrow account with the carrier. The carrier withholds $125
from each weekly settlement until the escrow balance reaches $2,500, and withholds again whenever
the balance falls below that amount.

6.2 Escrow is not withheld in any week in which the withholding would bring the owner-operator's
weekly net pay under $350.

6.3 The escrow balance is returned after the lease ends, less any amount the lease allows the
carrier to keep, with an itemized statement.

# 7. Fuel and company cards

7.1 Drivers may buy fuel with a carrier fuel card. Fuel an owner-operator buys on a carrier card is
deducted from that owner-operator's next settlement at the price paid.

7.2 A fuel card is for the assigned truck only, on dispatched trips. A purchase off the planned
route, larger than the truck's tanks hold, or on a day the truck is not under a load is reviewed
before it is allowed or deducted.

# 8. Detention billing to customers

8.1 Unless a customer's contract says otherwise, the carrier allows two hours free at each pickup
and each delivery, then bills detention at $60 per hour in 15-minute increments. A started increment
is billed in full.

8.2 Detention is measured from the arrival and departure times written on the bill of lading.
Drivers record both times at every stop; a stop missing either time cannot be billed.

# 9. Advances

9.1 A driver may ask for an advance against pay already earned and not yet paid. Each advance
carries a $3 fee.

9.2 Unless a driver's lease sets another limit, an advance may not exceed $600 or 50 percent of the
driver's earned and unpaid pay, whichever is less. The smallest advance is $75.

9.3 Advances and their fees are recovered from the driver's settlements, at most $300 per
settlement, until repaid.

9.4 No deduction of any kind may take a driver's weekly net pay under $300. When deductions would go
below that amount, the carrier holds back the rest and carries it to the next settlement.

# 10. Questions, corrections and changes

10.1 A driver who believes a settlement is wrong should tell the payroll office in writing within 30
days of the pay date. The carrier reviews every question and corrects a confirmed error on the next
settlement.

10.2 The carrier may change this policy with written notice before the pay week in which the change
takes effect. A change never applies to a pay week that has already begun.
